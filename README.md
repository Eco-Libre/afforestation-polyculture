# Eco-Libre Afforestation-Polyculture

This repo contains designs turning a grassy field into a forest.

<img src="afforestation-polyculture_first-generation.jpg?raw=true" alt="Export of first-generation.svg showing pioneer trees and polycultures for the afforestation of a grassy field"></a>

The process of turning a grassy field into a forest involves planting successive generations of polycultures. The first generation uses "pioneer" species, which serve a specific purpose (eg building biomass, nutirent accumulation, etc). These species will be cut down, mulched into the soil, and replaced by different species in subsequent generations.

# First Generation

The 'first-generation.svg' file shows what species and layout we plant in our polyculture when we first start work on a grassy field.

The first generation is the "pioneer" species' polyculture. The intention is to cut all of this down and replace it. The purpose is to build biomass, nutrient accumulation, and to shade the grass mostly out of existance.
    
Our First Generation Polyculture includes the following species:
    
* Mexican Sunflour
* Pinto Peanut
* Circropia
* Inga

Mexican Sunflour is *very* easy to propogate, and very easy to remove when-needed. It's a nutrient accumulator that quickly grows and shades the trees. It's the first thing that we plant in rows.

In this repo, exactly where we plant the mexican sunflour is indicated by the yellow dashes. The light green background shows the "buffer" around where it grows. This buffer is 4 meters in either direction from where we actually transplant the Mexican Sunflour.

Pinto Peanut is a short-growing ground cover and nitrogen fixer. It's purpose is to replace the grass at the base of where we will plant our pioneer trees.

In this repo, we indicate where we aggressively weed all the grass and replace it with Pinto Peanut with dark green squares that are 8 meters x 8 meters.

Inga and Circropia are our two pinoeer trees. Inga is a nitrogen fixer and Circropia is a fast growing tree that produces large amounts of large leaf litter.

In this repo, we indicate where to plant the pioneer trees with round black dots.
